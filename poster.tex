  \begin{block}{\chip{1} Abstract}

    Hill-type muscle models with switched activation dynamics have four directional linearizations at equilibrium. In a simulated elbow, choosing the wrong branch causes 14–75\% prediction error. In closed-loop MPC, relinearizing at the measured state reduces lowering overshoot from 0.37° to 0.02°, showing that branch selection matters for prediction and transient control.


  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{INTRODUCTION}

    Muscle models connect excitation to force in prostheses, exoskeletons, FES and tendon-driven robots. LQR and successive-linearization MPC use a local Jacobian to predict motion [1,2]. 
    
    But an equilibrium lies on two switches: excitation equals activation, and fibre velocity is zero. The perturbation direction selects one of four local models. 
    
    We quantify the prediction error and test how much of it survives feedback.


  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{RELATED WORK}

    Hill-type modelling. Thelen models activation and force generation; Millard et al. characterize computational muscle formulations [1,2].
    
    Smoothing. Kirsch et al. smooth torque–velocity dynamics; De Groote et al. smooth activation for optimization [3,4]. Here we measure the cost of branch choice at equilibrium.
    
    Non-smooth systems. Camlibel et al. give continuity conditions for bimodal linear systems [5]. Yeo et al. study a distinct force–length instability, not the activation-switch control problem [6].


  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{MODEL}

    Elbow joint, forearm $+$ hand pendulum (de Leva 1996), driven by
    \textbf{brachialis}: Thelen (2003) rigid-tendon Hill model, Holzbaur et
    al.\ (2005) parameters, moment arm digitized at pixel level from Murray et
    al.\ (1995) (PCHIP interpolation). Linearized at the gravity-only
    equilibrium $\theta^\ast = 60$\dg, $a^\ast = 0.113$: all four branches
    stable (eigenvalues $-99.7$ to $-0.3$/s); ZOH at $\mathbf{T_s = 10}$
    \textbf{ms} is well-conditioned on all four ($\max|A_d| \le 7.7$): not a
    stiffness problem, a non-smoothness problem.

    \begin{figure}
      \centering
      \includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{fig6_validation.pdf}
      \caption{Left: moment arm against Murray's independently measured
      specimen; peaks at 2.88\,cm at 110\dg, averages 2.18\,cm (Holzbaur's
      different model: 1.8\,cm). Right: brachialis peaks at 25.8\,\Nm\ near
      94\dg, about nine times the gravity load.}
    \end{figure}

    \textbf{Strength check:} brachialis, biceps and brachioradialis at full
    activation peak at 73.6\,\Nm\ at 94\dg; measured flexion strength
    (Buchanan) peaks at 79\,\Nm\ at 100\dg, RMS difference 3.6\,\Nm. A second
    data set (Amis) differs by 17.7\,\Nm\ RMS; the two measured sets differ
    from each other by 18\,\Nm.

  \end{block}

\end{column}

  \begin{block}{\chip{5} Result 1: the wrong branch costs 14 to 75\% of the signal}

    Linearization-based MPC holds one linearization over its horizon. From the
    equilibrium, apply a \textbf{small} excitation step ($+0.01$) and compare
    each branch's linear prediction with the true trajectory.

    \begin{figure}
      \centering
      \includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{figures/fig2_prediction.pdf}
      \caption{The matched branch tracks the truth almost exactly; the three
      mismatched branches diverge within 50 to 100\,ms.}
    \end{figure}

    \begin{table}
      \centering
      \small
      \begin{tabular}{lrrrrr}
        \toprule
        horizon & true swing & matched & wrong $a$ & wrong $\dot\theta$ & both\\
        \midrule
        50\,ms  & 0.116\dg & 1.1\% & \hi{71\%} & 14\%      & \hi{75\%}\\
        100\,ms & 0.468\dg & 1.3\% & \hi{57\%} & 24\%      & \hi{67\%}\\
        200\,ms & 1.497\dg & 1.2\% & 38\%      & 35\%      & \hi{58\%}\\
        500\,ms & 4.875\dg & 0.9\% & 14\%      & \hi{43\%} & \hi{51\%}\\
        \bottomrule
      \end{tabular}
      \caption{Final-angle error, \% of the true swing.}
    \end{table}

    In the small-signal regime, the wrong branch is 10 to 100$\times$ worse
    than linearization error itself. \textbf{It matters.}

  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{\chip{6} Result 2: at every equilibrium, not one}

    Swept gravity-only equilibria over the range of motion ($a^\ast$ stays in
    0.03 to 0.12: \textbf{posture is the low-activation regime}) and held-load
    equilibria $a^\ast = 0.02$ to $0.95$.

    \begin{figure}
      \centering
      \includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{figures/fig3_equilibria.pdf}
      \caption{Held-load sweep at 60\dg. The activation-branch error follows
      $\rho_a(a^\ast)$ exactly and vanishes at $a^\ast = 0.884$; the
      velocity-branch error grows to take its place.}
    \end{figure}

    \begin{itemize}
      \item Both branches wrong: \textbf{at least 23\% of an activating step}
        at every single-muscle equilibrium tested (deactivating: 14\% at
        500\,ms, held load).
      \item Deactivating steps: wrong-activation error \textbf{3.4$\times$
        larger} at 50\,ms (241\% vs 71\%): the wrong branch is then the
        \emph{faster} one.
    \end{itemize}

  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{\chip{7} Result 3: what survives feedback}

    Linear MPC at the reference equilibrium ($N = 20$, $T_s = 10$\,ms, same
    weights), true nonlinear plant, task 60 $\to$ 63 $\to$ 57\dg\ and a
    $\pm 3$\dg\ sinusoid; only the prediction model's \textbf{linearization}
    (branch and point) differs.

    \begin{figure}
      \centering
      \includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{figures/fig4_closedloop.pdf}
      \caption{Up: the blended model averages the fast and slow activation
      branches. Down: the sign test overshoots 0.37\dg, relinearization
      0.02\dg.}
    \end{figure}

    \begin{table}
      \centering
      \small
      \begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
        \toprule
        rule & up ovs. & settling & down ovs. & sin. RMS\\
        \midrule
        blended               & \hi{0.65} & \hi{0.23\,s} & 0.15      & 0.196\\
        smoothed act.         & \hi{0.42} & 0.21\,s      & \hi{0.48} & 0.169\\
        naive fixed           & 0.07      & 0.10\,s      & 0.00      & \hi{0.200}\\
        sign test             & 0.08      & 0.10\,s      & \hi{0.37} & 0.153\\
        \textbf{relinearize}  & 0.07      & 0.10\,s      & 0.02      & \textbf{0.149}\\
        \bottomrule
      \end{tabular}
      \caption{Angles in deg.}
    \end{table}

    Without delay, feedback removes stability and steady-state consequences,
    not transient ones. Raising step: blended overshoots \textbf{8 to
    9$\times$} as much as the bottom three rules (lowering: less than the sign
    test). Smoothed act.\ $=$ sign test with only the activation row averaged
    (De Groote's tanh row at equilibrium): 5$\times$ the sign test's raising
    overshoot, so \textbf{smoothing only the activation switch keeps most of
    the cost}. The sign test overshoots the lowering step (braking
    re-activates mid-plan); \textbf{relinearizing at the measured state}
    removes it.

  \end{block}

\end{column}
